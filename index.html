Quick Facts

Norway is a constitutional monarchy located in Northern Europe, on the Scandinavian Peninsula. It shares borders with Sweden, Finland, and Russia, and has a long coastline of almost 100,000 km (including fjords and islands). Although it is not a member of the EU, it belongs to the European Economic Area.

English in Norway

If you visit Norway, you can communicate in English almost everywhere, because most Norwegians learn it from an early age.

Norwegian Culture

Norwegian culture is deeply connected to nature and to the idea of equality. These concepts shape everyday life and social traditions:

friluftsliv — open-air living koselig — cozy dugnad — community work
  • "Hei!" — Hi! (informal greeting)
  • "Takk for maten" — Thanks for the food
  • "Skål!" — Cheers! (at celebrations)
Everyday customs

People usually take off their shoes when entering a home, and children often call adults by their first names. These habits reflect respect and equality.

"Ut på tur, aldri sur" — Out on a trip, never grumpy.

Economy & Welfare

Norway has a mixed economy with one of the strongest welfare states in the world. It is also one of the wealthiest countries per capita, thanks to its natural resources and careful management of public funds.

Sovereign Wealth Fund

The government fund had grown to over $1 trillion by 2020, making it the largest in the world. This fund supports free education, healthcare, and pensions.

However, the cost of living is very high. If you eat out in Oslo, you might pay twice as much as in many Latin American cities.

Education in Norway

Education is a priority in Norway. It is free at all levels, including university, and it promotes equality, critical thinking, and creativity.

  • 🎓 Free public education from primary school to university
  • 💰 Students can receive loans and grants from the state
  • 🧠 Interactive learning is more common than memorization
  • 👩‍🏫 Children call teachers by their first names
  • 🌍 Many students study abroad through exchange programs
Bilingual advantage

Most Norwegians speak fluent English because they start learning it at age six. If I had studied there, I would have learned Norwegian earlier!

Employment Opportunities

Norway offers excellent working conditions and one of the lowest unemployment rates in Europe (around 3–4%). Workers enjoy generous vacation, parental leave, and flexible hours.

Working in Norway

Foreigners may need to learn Norwegian for certain jobs. If you want to work in Norway, you should research the job market and validate your qualifications.

English is widely used in tech and business, so bilingual professionals have more opportunities.

Tourism in Norway

Norway is famous worldwide for its natural landscapes. Every year, millions of visitors travel to see the fjords, the Northern Lights, and the midnight sun.

Oslo Bergen Tromsø Lofoten Islands Geirangerfjord
  • 🚂 Ride the scenic Flåm Railway
  • 🐋 Go whale watching in Tromsø
  • 🛳️ Cruise through the fjords
  • 🎿 Ski in winter or hike in summer
Best time to visit

If you travel in winter, you must pack warm clothes. If you go in summer, you can experience the midnight sun. In my opinion, May and September offer fewer tourists and mild weather.

Conclusion

Norway is a fascinating country that combines natural beauty, social equality, and innovation. Its culture, strong economy, free education system, and tourism make it a unique example in the world.
